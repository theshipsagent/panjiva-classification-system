## Executive Summary

Comprehensive temporal analysis of Lower Mississippi River import cargo flows revealing declining import volumes (65.8M → 51.4M tons, -22%), strong seasonal patterns in fertilizers (3.05x) and chemicals (16.83x), and significant commodity-specific trends across petroleum, ferrous materials, and agricultural products.

**Key Finding:** Import tonnage declined 22% from 2023 to 2025, driven primarily by petroleum (-19%), chemicals (-91%), and steel (-68%) decreases, while forestry (+89%) and metals (+24%) showed strong growth.

## Strategic Insights

### 1. **Declining Import Trend Requires Investigation**

**22% decline over 3 years is significant:**
- Not explained by single commodity
- Multiple major commodities declining simultaneously
- Potential causes:
  - Domestic production substitution (steel, chemicals)
  - Supply chain diversification to other ports
  - Economic slowdown in Louisiana industrial base
  - Refinery operational changes

**Action Required:** Investigate whether this is permanent structural shift or temporary disruption.

### 2. **Chemicals Collapse is Critical Alert**

**91% decline is catastrophic:**
- 2023: 5.5M tons → 2025: 0.5M tons
- Not explained by seasonality (16.83x seasonality shows volatility, not collapse)
- Possible explanations:
  - Major chemical plant closure/curtailment
  - Supply chain re-routing to Houston/Gulf Coast
  - Data quality issue (missing 2025 data?)

**Action Required:** Immediate investigation of chemical import patterns, consignee analysis.

## Conclusion

The Lower Mississippi River experienced a **22% decline in import tonnage from 2023 to 2025** (65.8M → 51.4M tons), driven by petroleum (-19%), chemicals (-91%), and steel (-68%) declines. However, grain exports remain strong (1,160 vessel loadings in 2024), with **100% prediction coverage** validating the Phase 3 terminal visit prediction system.

**Seasonal patterns vary widely:** Fertilizers peak in March (3.05x seasonality) for pre-planting stockpiling, while petroleum shows minimal seasonality (1.68x) due to continuous refinery operations. **Terminal visits surge 18% in Q4** (Sept-Nov) driven by grain harvest exports.

**Critical Alert:** The 91% chemicals decline from 2023 to 2025 requires immediate investigation to determine if this represents a permanent supply chain shift or data quality issue.

**Next Priority:** Investigate 2025 data completeness, analyze chemicals collapse, and obtain export manifest to validate grain export tonnage estimates.
