## REGIONAL TRADE ROUTES DISCOVERED

### 1. Nova Scotia → Florida Aggregate Pipeline
- **Auld's Cove, NS** (99.9% aggregates) → **Tampa, FL** (40.5% construction materials)
- 5.3M tons crushed stone flow
- Feeds Florida construction boom

### 2. Venezuelan Crude → Gulf Coast
- **Jose, Venezuela** (90.7% crude) → **Pascagoula, MS** (76.7% crude)
- Also to New Orleans, Port Arthur
- Heavy crude requiring complex refining

### 3. Brazilian Steel → US East Coast
- **Sepetiba Bay, Brazil** (53.2% steel) → Philadelphia, Baltimore, Charleston
- 5.8M tons steel exports to US
- Competes with domestic steel production

### 4. Rotterdam Chemicals → New York/Newark
- **Rotterdam** (88.3% chemicals) → **NY/NJ** (28.6% chemicals)
- 12M tons European chemical exports
- Feeds Northeast chemical industry

### 5. Iraqi Crude → California
- **Basra, Iraq** → **Long Beach, CA** (20.6% crude)
- 2.3M tons Middle East crude to West Coast
- Alternative to Alaskan crude

### 6. Aruba Transshipment → Texas
- **Orangestad, Aruba** (99% crude) → **Port Arthur, TX** (80.3% crude)
- 3.4M tons via Caribbean transshipment
- Masks Venezuelan crude origin

## MOST SURPRISING FINDINGS

1. **Richmond > Port Arthur:** Richmond, CA (84.5%) is MORE specialized in crude than Port Arthur, TX (80.3%)

2. **Gramercy Cement Mystery:** Port of Gramercy shows 83.7% "General Cargo" - investigation reveals this is bulk cement from Vietnam, misclassified in taxonomy

3. **Philadelphia > Houston:** Philadelphia is MORE diversified (11 commodities, HHI=0.22) than Houston (14 commodities, HHI=0.23)

4. **Auld's Cove Mono-Commodity:** A major port (5.3M tons) with 99.99% single commodity (aggregates) - rarest specialization pattern

5. **San Juan Energy Dependency:** Puerto Rico imports 40.3% petroleum products (3.6M tons) - critical energy vulnerability

6. **Aruba Pure Transshipment:** 99% crude flow through Aruba with no other activity - pure maritime logistics hub

## IMPLICATIONS FOR FUTURE ANALYSIS

### Supply Chain Vulnerabilities
- San Juan's petroleum dependence (40.3% of tonnage)
- Gulf Coast crude concentration (5 ports = 48M tons)
- Single-source aggregate routes (NS → Tampa)

### Refinery Capacity Mapping
- Match crude import ports to refinery locations
- Richmond, Port Arthur, Pascagoula = refinery-adjacent
- Seasonal crude import patterns (winter vs summer demand)

### Trade Route Optimization
- Why do Iraqi/Brazilian crude choose West Coast vs Gulf?
- Transport cost modeling: Venezuela → Gulf vs West Coast
- Crude quality matching (heavy Venezuelan vs light Iraqi)

### Port Competition Analysis
- Philadelphia vs Baltimore (both diversified East Coast)
- Long Beach vs LA (both Pacific crude gateways)
- New Orleans vs Houston (Gulf diversified hubs)
