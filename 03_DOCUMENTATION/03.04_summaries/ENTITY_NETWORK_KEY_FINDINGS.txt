KEY INSIGHTS
================================================================================

1. EXCLUSIVITY DOMINATES (70% single-customer shippers)
   → Dedicated supply chain relationships over spot markets
   → Long-term contracts common (Bolanter → Saudi Refining 100%)
   → Supply chain vulnerability if single source fails

2. MODERATE CONCENTRATION (Top 10 = 30-40%)
   → Not dominated by oligopoly (cf. 50%+ in highly concentrated markets)
   → Mix of majors (ExxonMobil, Chevron) and specialists (PMI Trading)
   → Competitive dynamics possible despite large players

3. MULTI-ROLE ENTITIES CONTROL DISPROPORTIONATE FLOWS
   → 1,290 entities (2.5%) appear in 2+ roles
   → Petroleum majors handle 100M+ tons across all roles (S+C+N)
   → Vertical integration = lower transaction costs + market intelligence

4. TRADING INTERMEDIARIES ADD 50M+ TONS OF FLOW
   → PMI Trading: 40.8M tons shipped (7% of total network tonnage)
   → Connects NOCs (SOMO, PDVSA) to U.S. refiners
   → Hub-and-spoke topology: PMI as hub, refiners as spokes

5. SELF-CLEARANCE INDICATES SOPHISTICATION
   → Chevron, Valero clear own 40M+ ton shipments (C=N pattern)
   → High-volume = self-clear, Low-volume = 3PL broker
   → Petroleum = self-clear, Machinery = dedicated broker

6. INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC NETWORK TOPOLOGIES
   → Petroleum: Hub-and-spoke (centralized trading via PMI/Trafigura)
   → Bauxite: Point-to-point (Orca → EGA dedicated supply)
   → Automotive: Tree structure (Toyota Corp → Toyota Sales → dealers)
   → Cement: Regional clusters (Asian cement → West Coast ports)

7. INTERNAL TRANSFERS = 64M TONS (11.5% of network)
   → 8 of top 20 relationships are internal (Chevron → Chevron)
   → Indicates inter-facility movements (crude → refinery → product)
   → Tolling arrangements (ship crude, receive refined products)

8. GEOPOLITICAL RISK CONCENTRATION
   → Iraqi crude: 15.5M tons (SOMO → U.S. refiners)
   → Venezuelan crude: 13.7M tons (PDVSA/Petropiar → Chevron)
   → Supply chain exposed to sanctions, production disruptions

================================================================================
CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The 2024 U.S. maritime import network is characterized by strategic exclusivity
(70% dedicated relationships) combined with moderate concentration (top 10 = 30-40%).

Multi-role entities (Chevron, Valero, ExxonMobil) dominate with 100M+ tons across
shipper, consignee, and notify party roles, demonstrating vertical integration and
sophisticated logistics operations.

Trading intermediaries (PMI Trading, Trafigura) serve as critical hubs connecting
national oil companies to U.S. refiners in a hub-and-spoke petroleum network,
while bulk commodities (bauxite, phosphate) operate on dedicated point-to-point
supply chains.

Industry-specific topologies reflect rational economic behavior:
  - Petroleum: Requires liquidity → Hub-and-spoke trading
  - Bauxite: Requires reliability → Dedicated suppliers
  - Automotive: Requires distribution → Tree structure (OEM → dealers)
  - Cement: Requires regional efficiency → Port-proximate clusters

This network structure balances efficiency (dedicated relationships reduce search
costs), resilience (diversified sourcing mitigates risk), and flexibility (trading
intermediaries provide spot market access).
